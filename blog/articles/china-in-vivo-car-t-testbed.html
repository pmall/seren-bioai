Protein to Pill

The in vivo testbed is China

29 September 2026

Of the roughly 140 in vivo CAR-T drugs in animal or human testing worldwide, 82% were developed by groups in China. The foundational preclinical work mostly happened in the US and Germany. The move into people mostly happened in China. In vivo cell therapy — engineering immune cells inside the body instead of in a factory — now has a geography: tested in China, imported by everyone else.

How the fast path works

The mechanism is the investigator-initiated-trial fast path, which lets Chinese hospitals move cell therapies into patients without the regulator's supervision. No multi-year IND negotiation, no centralized manufacturing audit before first-in-human — a hospital ethics review and a willing principal investigator can be enough. That speed is why a concept that was a pivot slide in Boston in early September — ArsenalBio halting all ex vivo work to bet entirely on in vivo, TScan cutting three-quarters of its staff to do the same — was already a clinical queue in Shanghai and Shenzhen.

This is not a story about China out-inventing anyone. It is a story about who absorbs the first-in-human risk. The West did the vector biology and the mouse models; China is running the tolerability experiments at population scale.

What has worked so far

The early readouts are autoimmune, not oncology. Five lupus patients improved at three months in a September 2025 report, and sixteen patients across autoimmune conditions improved in September 2026 updates, without serious adverse events reported. Small numbers, short follow-up, investigator-reported — but directionally the thing the field needed: in vivo-generated CAR-T cells finding their target and quieting disease without the manufacturing chain.

The bridge back to the US opened this month: the first China-developed in vivo CAR-T was cleared by the US FDA for testing, in blood cancers. Discovery risk taken in one regulatory system, evidence imported into another. If that clearance becomes a pattern — Chinese IIT signal, American registrational trial — the industry's in vivo pipeline starts to look like its antibody pipeline did five years ago: invented anywhere, validated in China, sold globally.

Why it might not transfer

Two caveats carry the whole story. First, durability is unproven and the toxicities that sank the ex vivo autoimmune push have not been shown to stay behind. The ex vivo reckoning was specific: Novartis suspended eight autoimmune and neurology trials after three deaths from IEC-HS, and BMS paused its own program the same day. In vivo delivery removes the factory but not the biology — an expanding immune effector inside the body can still expand too far, and a few months of improvement in a handful of patients says nothing about five-year persistence. The counterpoint that ex vivo durability is real where toxicity is tolerable — 10 of 20 myeloma patients alive and progression-free past five years on Carvykti — sharpens the question rather than answering it: persistence works, but at what inflammatory price in people who are not dying of cancer?

Second, the fast path itself is the swing variable. The same analysis that counts the 82% notes regulatory tightening could slow the IIT route. If Beijing decides investigator-led cell therapy needs full regulator supervision, the testbed narrows just as Western programs arrive to use it. The parallel to watch is the broader industrial plan — Merck paying up to $2.13B for a China-originated preclinical KRAS glue the same week — which suggests pharma already treats China as originator supply, not just a trial site. Trial capacity and molecule supply would then tighten together.

What to watch

Whether the first FDA-cleared China-developed candidate enrolls on schedule and what its first Western safety data show; whether any in vivo autoimmune readout reaches a year of follow-up with B cell aplasia and off-therapy remission intact; and whether the IIT rules tighten, loosen, or split — a supervised path for first-in-human and a fast path for follow-ons would keep the testbed open while answering the durability question. Until then, the in vivo pivot has an address, and it is not Boston.
