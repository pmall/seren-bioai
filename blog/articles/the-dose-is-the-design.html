Protein to Pill

The dose is the design

5 October 2026

Dyno Therapeutics presented AI-designed muscle capsids that work at 5e12 vg/kg — roughly 20 times below the typical ~1e14 vg/kg systemic AAV dose — in a late-breaker at the World Muscle Society congress. One capsid showed ~20x biodistribution and ~40x transduction versus MyoAAV-4E in non-human primates, with a second capsid showing ~10x lower liver biodistribution. In mice the gain was ~2x across skeletal muscle. The work is preclinical only. The interesting part is what it attacks.

What multi-receptor means

Multi-receptor here means the capsids were designed to engage more than one cell-entry receptor, rather than relying on a single attachment pathway. The stated goal is cross-species translatability by design — selected in mice and primates together, instead of a mouse-optimized capsid that fades in primates. That failure mode has killed more than one muscle program, so designing against it explicitly matters more than the fold-improvement numbers.

Liver detargeting matters just as much. Systemic AAV toxicity — complement, liver injury, high-dose deaths — scales with total vector load and with where the vector goes besides muscle. A capsid that holds muscle transduction at a 20x lower dose while cutting liver uptake goes at the safety leg directly, rather than asking regulators and payers to tolerate the old dose.

Why this lands now

Gene therapy just had its busiest validation month: Ultragenyx won two firsts — Fayuvi for Sanfilippo A and Genglycos for GSDIa, each with a ~$200M priority-review voucher, then took the Sanfilippo program transatlantic with an EMA filing noting a September 17 FDA approval. At the same meeting where Dyno presented, Sarepta's Elevidys showed durable 2-year benefit in 25 ambulatory 8–12-year-olds, with 20% treatment-related events and 3 early liver events — and uniQure's Huntington's program showed a 4-year effect-size wobble from 75% to 44% slowing when the external control was updated.

Put together, the third wave has two open risks: evidence fragility (durability judged against external controls that can move) and dose toxicity (liver and complement at ~1e14). Dyno answers the second, not the first. That is the right division of labor — you cannot statistics your way out of a dose problem.

Infrastructure, not a pipeline

Dyno's partners on the program include Astellas and Roche, plus NVIDIA — a pharma pair plus a compute vendor, which is the standard shape for AI delivery work now. Capsids are infrastructure: one good muscle capsid with liver detargeting improves every transgene put inside it, the way a better LNP improves every RNA payload. That is why delivery advances get licensed (Frontiers is named on this one) rather than built into single-asset companies.

What to watch

Three questions decide whether this is a platform or a poster. First, does the primate gain survive a therapeutic transgene — reporter transduction overstates what a large dystrophin or micro-dystrophin cassette delivers. Second, does lower dose actually lower complement and liver risk in a dosed toxicology species, not just biodistribution. Third, which partner moves first into a clinic, and with what indication: a rare monogenic muscle disease where a small efficacy signal reads clearly, or a larger program where manufacturing at 5e12 changes the cost equation.

Until then, the week's lesson is narrow but load-bearing: the AAV field spent years arguing about controls and durability while dosing around the toxicity. Designing the capsid to need less vector is the more direct fix.
