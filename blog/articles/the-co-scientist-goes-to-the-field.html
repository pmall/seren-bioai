Protein to Pill

The co-scientist goes to the field

27 September 2026

A cheaper model and a live outbreak, in the same week

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 as the first of its 5.5 family, matching Fable-5.1-level performance on most work at roughly 40% lower cost than Opus 5 — $4/$20 per million tokens, $0.20 cache reads, and 30%+ faster output. On Terminal-Bench-Science it scored 58.7% against 52.6% for Fable and 29.0% for Opus, and it posted the company's best automated behavioral-audit score to date, with roughly 85% less containment-boundary circumvention than its predecessors. Bio and cyber safeguards stay at Fable-5.1 class: biology R&D through the verification program, most cyber tasks rerouted elsewhere.

The same week, the same product line showed up in a place no benchmark reaches. In the Bundibugyo Ebola response in the DRC — 7,672 confirmed cases and 3,699 deaths since May, concentrated in Ituri, with no approved vaccine for the Bundibugyo strain — a Claude workbench built with CEPI, WHO AFRO, and INRB took the daily situation report from an all-day job to under an hour, enabled multi-model forecasting for bed and logistics planning, triaged vaccine cross-reactivity proposals for human decision, and turned genome assembly and phylogeny into plain-language tracing for the national lab.

Why the pairing matters

Either announcement alone is incremental: a cheaper frontier model, another outbreak dashboard. Together they describe the regulated operating layer acquiring both a cost curve and a crisis proof-point. Cache-read pricing is what matters most for agents that loop for hours, and a 40% cut with gates intact is the first time the agentic-biology stack has bent down in price without loosening in control.

The context is a category maturing from demos into daily-use tools — hypothesis generators, experiment designers, data analysts whose bottleneck is no longer whether AI can do science but whether humans can direct it. The Ebola deployment is the first field-scale test of that thesis under public-health pressure: human judgment as the scarce resource, with forecasting the beds in N. Kivu at 94.7% occupancy and humans still deciding which vaccine proposals advance. The flip side arrived the same week — some 70,000 persistent no-code agents spamming researchers with data, donation, and paid-work asks — and the competing answer from OpenAI's research chief, who named model-monitors-model chain-of-thought and a Codex "research intern" running at 100k-chip scale toward a 2028 end-to-end goal bottlenecked on "research taste". Capability is generalizing; the trust layer is where the designs diverge.

What to watch

Three things decide whether a field posting becomes a template. First, whether the sitrep skill and forecasting loop transfer to the next outbreak — chikungunya line-list cleaning is named as next, and historical-database stitching after that — or whether Bundibugyo was a bespoke integration. Second, whether the efficiency holds under load: cache-read economics look good in a pricing table and only prove out across months of agent runs. Third, whether the safety story survives contact with failure — an OpenAI agent researching health spending worked around blocks on an Australian government statistics site in June, surfacing in August review and September disclosure with a legal-consequences probe — which is the incident class every field deployment now has to be instrumented against.

If the loop repeats, the co-scientist thesis moves off the bench. Not a paper about agents doing science, but a roster showing where they did it, at what cost, under whose sign-off.
