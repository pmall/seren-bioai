Protein to Pill

The combo becomes the standard

7 October 2026

AbbVie and Genmab's Epkinly (epcoritamab, CD20xCD3) plus R-CHOP cut the risk of progression or death by 51% versus R-CHOP alone in frontline diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, in a Phase 3 of about 900 patients. The benefit was consistent across risk groups, and the companies plan filings next. It is the first Phase 3 win for a bispecific-plus-chemo combination in frontline lymphoma.

Cleared a bar nobody had cleared

The analyst bar going in was a 30–35% risk reduction — roughly the level at which a new frontline regimen actually displaces R-CHOP, a backbone that has absorbed every challenger for two decades. The two most recent attempts framed the difficulty: Polivy's 27% and Monjuvi's 25%, neither enough to rewrite the standard. Fifty-one percent is not a marginal beat. It is nearly double the prior best, in the largest test of the bispecific-combo idea to date.

R-CHOP here means rituximab plus cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone — the four-decade-old chemo-immunotherapy backbone that still cures roughly three in five frontline patients and fails the rest. Adding a CD20xCD3 T-cell engager — one arm grabbing the lymphoma cell, the other dragging in a killer T cell — was supposed to deepen first remission without the logistics of cell therapy. Until this readout, that was a thesis. Now there is a number attached.

Value moves to format plus combo

The timing matters beyond lymphoma. Days earlier, Summit, AstraZeneca, and Daiichi Sankyo hardened the ivonescimab-plus-Datroway pairing into a sponsored solid-tumor program starting in first-line triple-negative breast cancer, with each party keeping its asset. Different disease, different bispecific (PD-1xVEGF), different partner (a TROP2 antibody-drug conjugate instead of chemo) — same commercial shape: the bispecific is no longer sold as a single agent looking for share, but as a combination anchor.

That is the same lesson AI bispecific discovery just taught on the supply side: Earendil selling to both Sanofi and Genentech, Chai licensing the same antibody-design models to four pharmas — discovery priced as shared infrastructure, differentiation downstream. Epkinly completes the arc on the demand side. The molecule was already approved; what this trial prices is the regimen — engager plus backbone, tested head-to-head against the backbone alone, winning by enough to change prescribing.

What to watch

Three questions decide how far this travels. First, safety at frontline scale: engager toxicities (cytokine release, immune-effector events, cytopenias stacked on chemo) were manageable in relapsed disease, but 900 frontline patients will be read closely for anything that offsets a progression-free survival gain. Second, whether the benefit holds in overall survival with longer follow-up — regulators will file on PFS, but guidelines and payers anoint on survival. Third, how fast the combo template replicates: PD-1xVEGF plus ADC in solid tumors is now in the clinic on the same logic, and every CD3 engager with a chemo backbone behind it just got a friendlier precedent to cite.

Until then, the narrow lesson is load-bearing for the whole bispecific class: after years of arguing about formats and discovery engines, the first frontline win came from pairing the format with the oldest backbone in the book — and beating it by half.
