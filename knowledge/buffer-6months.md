# Buffer — six months

This is the long-term memory of the field. It holds what would change
someone's reading of the whole field, not just this week: the events that
actually mattered, and the longer theses and hypotheses that are playing out
over months. It is the editorial line — when the short-term buffer says what's
happening, this file says what it means and where it's heading.

It is bounded, but by significance rather than age. An entry stays as long as
it carries weight and goes when it doesn't — a thesis that played out, an
impact that faded, a shift that got superseded by something more important.
New impactful developments are welcome to displace older, weaker ones. Keep
it to the dozen or two things that genuinely shape the field.

Every entry carries the exact source URL it came from, and a hypothesis
records the observation it grew out of.

## Theses and hypotheses

- **Compute is becoming the rate limiter, not the accelerant.** Arm CEO (Sep 2026) names chip supply as the constraint on AI cancer/drug-discovery ambitions; Generate CTO counters that GPUs alone are insufficient without proprietary bio-data + experimentation. SK Biopharm 128×B200, Novo+AWS, BioHive-2, JapanFold/Tenstorrent all race to secure sovereign or proprietary compute. Hypothesis: the next moat is fabs + bio-data + wet-lab capacity jointly, and supply-constrained HBM/compute delays smaller players most. (Observation: Arm/Benzinga Sep 8 2026; Generate/BMS NVIDIA Sep 2026; SK Biopharm, Novo-AWS, JapanFold Sep 2026)

- **The generalization gap is the real bottleneck.** AlphaFold 3 was transformative for known structural families, but drug discovery lives in unexplored biological space. IsoDDE's step change on out-of-distribution benchmarks suggests the field's next frontier is not better predictions on familiar systems, but reliable predictions on novel ones. Deep Origin's DODock now shows this concretely: co-folding models drop below 25% pose accuracy on novel targets, while DODock maintains >50%. The Stanford AI Index 2026 quantifies the binding success gap: 85.87% expression but only 9.65% binding — designs fold correctly but don't reliably bind targets. (Observation: IsoDDE benchmark performance, Feb 2026; Deep Origin DODock, Aug 2026; Stanford AI Index Aug 2026)

- **The Eroom's Law counter-thesis.** AI-native companies claim 80-90% Phase 1 success vs. 40-65% industry average, but Phase 2 rates are comparable (~40%). Rentosertib Phase III is the first real test: if it fails, the thesis that AI accelerates discovery without improving clinical outcomes strengthens. If it succeeds, the counter-thesis weakens. (Observation: BCG data via Fortune, April 2025; rentosertib Phase III, Aug 2026)

- **Three platform models are crystallizing.** (1) IsoDDE: structural biology → drug design (compute-heavy, pharma partnerships). (2) Recursion: phenomics data → full pipeline (massive wet lab + compute, own pipeline + partnerships). (3) Insilico: end-to-end AI-first (target ID → molecule → trial prediction, own pipeline). Each bets on a different bottleneck. (Observation: company websites and Fortune profile)

- **Open-source as competitive strategy is dominant.** Aureka's OpenDDE outperforms AlphaFold 3 on antibody-antigen tasks (76.1% DockQ vs 47.9%) and is Apache 2.0 licensed. Boltz-2, used by all top 20 pharma, is MIT-licensed and approaches FEP accuracy at 1000x speed. ESMFold2 surpasses AF3 on complex prediction. The moat is proprietary experimental data — not model weights. This "release the model, keep the data" pattern is now the dominant competitive strategy in AI drug discovery. (Observation: Aureka Aug 2026, Boltz-2 ongoing, ESMFold2 May 2026)

- **AI agents are entering the clinical workflow layer — and regulators are building infrastructure to accommodate them.** Pathos AI's Foundry uses thousands of AI agents to design trials, match patients, and optimize dosing — not just discover molecules. Schrödinger's Bunsen makes computational chemistry accessible to non-specialists through natural language. CluePoints launches AI tools for clinical data review built inside a top 10 pharma's live portfolio. The bottleneck is shifting from "find molecules" to "prove they work efficiently in the right patients." FDA + Duke-Margolis convened QMIN for modeling/simulation in drug development. The regulatory infrastructure is evolving to accommodate AI-augmented clinical development. (Observation: Pathos AI, Schrödinger BMS deal, Aug 2026; CluePoints Sep 2026; FDA QMIN Sep 2026)

- **The infrastructure layer is consolidating.** Schrödinger (physics-based), Anthropic (workflow/integration), NVIDIA BioNeMo (ecosystem), and cloud providers (AWS/Novo Nordisk) are building the operating systems for AI drug discovery. BMS deploying Bunsen at scale signals big pharma is betting on physics-based computation augmented by AI, not just AI alone. (Observation: BMS-Schrödinger deal, Novo Nordisk-AWS, Claude Science, Aug 2026)

- **AI drug discovery may produce the first longevity therapeutics.** Rentosertib — an AI-designed drug for an AI-discovered target — now shows unanimous age reversal across six independent proteomic aging clocks in a clinical trial. The effect is partially independent of disease improvement, suggesting the drug has genuine geroprotective activity. This establishes a dual-purpose trial framework that could accelerate longevity drug discovery by embedding aging biomarkers into standard disease trials. (Observation: Insilico rentosertib Nature Biotechnology Sep 2026; Longevity Vaccines Sep 2026)

- **The autoimmune cell therapy safety reckoning is here.** Novartis and BMS both paused autoimmune CAR-T programs within days — Novartis after 3 deaths, BMS after inflammatory events. The field expanded CAR-T into autoimmune diseases expecting the oncology safety profile would hold, but IEC-HS in a non-oncology population with different immune baselines is proving more dangerous. Roche's same-day $75M bet on a preclinical trispecific antibody for the same B cell biology signals capital is already rotating away from ex vivo cell therapy toward off-the-shelf approaches. (Observation: Novartis/BMS CAR-T pauses, Roche-Simcere deal, Sep 2026)

- **De novo protein design for small molecules crosses a threshold.** NISE (Fry et al., Nature Jun 2026) achieves 100% (exatecan) and 83% (apixaban) via LASErMPNN+RFAA closed-loop selection-expansion. Ligand self-consistency proves far stricter than backbone-only. This is no longer proof-of-concept — it's a working recipe with implications for drug delivery, biosensors, and therapeutic proteins. (Observation: Nature NISE paper, Jun 2026)

- **Protein engineering is entering an alignment era.** ProteinDPO demonstrates that alignment techniques from language models (DPO) can be applied to protein language models for generalizable stability prediction. Raygun shows proteins can be miniaturized or expanded while preserving function. Function-aware masking achieves 14% gain on structure tasks by aligning pretraining corruption with functional priors. AAMFM uses Calibrated DPO for antigen-specific antibody design. These mirror the broader AI industry shift from supervised learning to alignment techniques. (Observation: ProteinDPO, Aug 2026; Raygun, Jul 2026; function-aware masking Sep 2026; AAMFM Jul 2026)

- **The field is bifurcating on validation.** Insilico's DDD Benchmark and the Nature Reviews perspective both demand that AI prove clinical decision-making value, not just model performance. The gap between "impressive demos" and "approved medicines" remains the central tension. With ~$60B invested, ~175 programs in trials, and zero FDA approvals, the field's credibility now hinges on Phase III results, not benchmark scores. (Observation: Insilico DDD BaaS, Nature Reviews Drug Discovery perspective, Aug 2026)

- **AI drug discovery capital is dangerously concentrated.** Isomorphic ($2.1B) + Chai ($400M) = 95% of H1 2026 AI drug discovery funding. The remaining ~$130M was spread across 10 companies. However, the broader biotech capital picture is healthier: US/European biopharma startups raised $12.6B in H1 2026 (five-year high), with family offices increasingly active. The sector-level "boom" masks a reality where most AI-native companies are capital-starved. (Observation: NewMarketPitch Aug 2026, Citeline Aug 2026, CNBC Sep 2026)

- **General-purpose AI models are entering protein design — but per-target variance is extreme.** Anthropic's Claude achieved 22-35% hit rates designing protein binders against 15 targets, validated wet lab by Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience. However, Towards AI's reanalysis reveals the headline 26.8% pooled rate masks extreme between-target variance: per-target rates ranged from 80% to 0%. A 30-design batch has 20% chance of returning zero binders. This is distinct from specialized protein AI — a general reasoning model performing domain-specific work. (Observation: Anthropic Aug 2026, Adaptyv Bio Aug 2026, Towards AI Sep 2026)

- **Open-source protein models are catching up to proprietary ones.** ESMFold2 surpasses AlphaFold3 on complex prediction and is fully MIT-licensed. Aureka's OpenDDE outperforms AF3 on antibody-antigen tasks. The open-source trajectory suggests that proprietary model advantages may be temporary, shifting the moat to proprietary data and experimental validation infrastructure. (Observation: ESMFold2, May 2026; Aureka OpenDDE, Aug 2026)

- **Protein design is expanding beyond static structure.** VibeGen (MIT, Mar 2026) demonstrates that proteins can be designed by their dynamics — vibration, flexing, shape-shifting — not just their folded shape. This "functional degeneracy" opens a vast new design space. Combined with ProteinDPO and Raygun, the field is moving from structure prediction to programmable molecular machines. (Observation: VibeGen, Mar 2026; ProteinDPO, Aug 2026; Raygun, Jul 2026)

- **In vivo cell therapy is the next frontier as ex vivo hits walls.** ArsenalBio halted all ex vivo CAR T for solid tumors, pivoting entirely to in vivo CAR T. TScan cut 75% of staff, pausing Phase 3 and autoimmune program, pivoting to in vivo TCR-T. Ex vivo cell therapy for solid tumors is proving commercially and technically unviable — manufacturing complexity, T cell exhaustion, poor tumor infiltration — forcing a generational shift to in vivo engineering. (Observation: ArsenalBio Sep 2 2026, TScan Sep 3 2026)

- **Pharma is building infrastructure layers, not just pipelines.** Lilly TuneLab ($1B+ data, federated learning), NVIDIA BioNeMo ecosystem, and BMS-Schrödinger partnership signal that big pharma is investing in the operating system for AI drug discovery, not just individual molecules. TuneLab's federated model — where biotechs run models locally, share only model updates — may become the template for how pharma shares AI capabilities without exposing IP. (Observation: Lilly TuneLab, Twist-TuneLab Sep 2026; NVIDIA-Lilly co-innovation lab, Jan 2026)

- **The antibody design bottleneck is collapsing.** Germinal achieves epitope-targeted de novo antibody design with nanomolar affinities using only 43-101 experimental tests per antigen. AIntibody benchmark establishes the first CASP-like blinded standard. Chai-2 achieves 20% zero-shot hit rates with 5 major pharma partnerships. Stanford AI Index quantifies the remaining gap: 85.87% expression but only 9.65% binding. The question is no longer "can AI design antibodies?" but "which platform produces clinical candidates?" (Observation: Germinal Jun 2026, AIntibody Aug 2026, Chai partnerships Jul-Aug 2026, Stanford AI Index Aug 2026)

- **Two commercial models are crystallizing for AI drug discovery.** (1) High-volume licensing: Insilico generates 33+ preclinical candidates and partners at preclinical stage, now forecasting profitability. (2) Platform-to-pipeline: Isomorphic, Generate, NewLimit build their own pipelines. Chai represents a third variant: shared infrastructure licensing — same models licensed non-exclusively to 4 large pharmas. The profitability signal from Insilico suggests the high-volume licensing model may reach commercial proof before platform-to-pipeline competitors. (Observation: Insilico CEO interview BioSpace Aug 2026; Insilico H1 forecast Jul 2026; Chai partnerships Jul-Aug 2026)

- **Closed-loop protein engineering is crossing the threshold from proof-of-concept to practical tool.** ORI demonstrates 100-fold lysozyme activity improvements through ontology-conditioned generation with RL from experimental feedback. Matwings demonstrated 4 iterative AI–wet lab cycles across 222 protein variants for an ophthalmic biologic, achieving clinical clearance in China — first clinical candidate from a closed-loop protein AI workflow. The pattern: computational redesign → experimental feedback → further optimization produces outcomes that neither approach achieves alone. (Observation: ORI Mar 2026, Matwings Aug 2026)
