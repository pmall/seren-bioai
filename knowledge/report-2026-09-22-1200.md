# Report — 2026-09-22

## Findings

- **2026-09-21** — Nature technology feature on AI co-scientists: comprehensive survey of the emerging category of AI research assistants — Google Co-Scientist, FutureHouse Kosmos, Phylo, Huawei's platform, Sakana AI — that generate hypotheses, design experiments, and analyze data autonomously via multi-agent reasoning. Key case studies: (1) Whitehead Institute's Anna Pertl used Co-Scientist to find a novel MYC-targeting strategy — instead of dissolving condensates (Dewpoint's approach), the AI proposed gluing them together via click chemistry to shut down the cancer gene, an approach the lab had never considered. (2) Imperial College London researchers (Penadés & Costa) found Co-Scientist independently reproduced their years-long bacterial DNA transfer theory in ~2 days from public literature alone. The article's central insight: as AI handles more discovery mechanics, the scarce resource becomes human scientific judgment — knowing which questions are worth asking. "The most valuable part now is actually asking the question" (Ajay Agrawal, Toronto). The paradox: researchers may need deeper expertise to evaluate AI outputs even as students get fewer opportunities to build that expertise. Signals: the AI co-scientist category is maturing from demo to daily-use tool; the bottleneck is shifting from "can AI do science?" to "can humans effectively direct AI science?" — a training and judgment problem, not a compute problem. https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02931-5

- **2026-09-18** — First RNA therapy improvement in rare ALS (*Nature*, *Med* journal): a man with CHCHD10-mutation ALS (affecting <1% of inherited ALS) showed improved motor symptoms and normalized neurofilament light chain levels one year after receiving an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) therapy. Blood NfL dropped to normal reference range, suggesting neuronal preservation. Motor skill scores improved; breathing and cognition stable. The drug took only 3 years to develop (vs. typical decade+ for ASO therapies), targeting a rare/unique mutation. Signals: mutation-specific ASO development timeline compressed from decade to 3 years; proof-of-concept for personalized RNA therapeutics in neurodegeneration; the model of "one patient, one drug" may become viable for rare genetic ALS subtypes. https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02945-z / https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medj.2026.101295
